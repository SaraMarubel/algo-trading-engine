How the algorithm decides

This trader combines three widely-used technical-analysis building blocks:

  • Moving average crossover. When the fast SMA rises above the slow SMA (a "golden cross"), it's a standard momentum signal that short-term price action has turned bullish relative to the intermediate trend; the reverse ("death cross") signals bearish momentum. A 9/21 pairing is a faster variant of the classic 50/200-day golden/death cross used for longer-term signals.
  • RSI filter (Wilder, 1978). The Relative Strength Index measures the speed and magnitude of recent price moves over a lookback period (14 by default). Readings above 70 are conventionally "overbought," below 30 "oversold." Used here to avoid buying into an already-extended move, and to help trigger profit-taking.
  • Fixed-fractional position sizing with a stop-loss / take-profit bracket. Each entry risks a fixed % of available cash (scales with account size rather than a flat share count), and every position carries a symmetric-ish risk bracket that exits automatically if price moves against or in favor of the trade by a set %.
  • Re-entry cooldown. After an exit, the algorithm won't open a new position in the same ticker for a set number of seconds — a simple form of the hysteresis real systematic strategies use to avoid "overtrading" (repeatedly flipping a position on noise and paying the spread/slippage each time).

Large manual orders use a separate concept — TWAP execution — which slices an order over time instead of routing it all at once, to reduce market impact. See the ⓘ next to the Dump/Accumulate buttons.
